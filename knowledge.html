Engineering Fundamentals

MECHANICAL

Newton's Second Law

Understand the relationship between force, mass and acceleration.

Newton's second law states that the net force acting on an object equals its mass multiplied by its acceleration.
F = ma
Where:
F = force (N)
m = mass (kg)
a = acceleration (m/s²)

This relationship is fundamental to vehicle dynamics, robotics, mechanisms and machine design.
MECHANICAL

Stress & Strain

Learn how engineering materials respond to applied loading.

Stress describes the internal force per unit cross-sectional area.
σ = F / A
Strain describes deformation relative to the original dimension.
ε = ΔL / L
For a linear elastic material:
E = σ / ε
E is Young's modulus.
ELECTRICAL

Ohm's Law

The fundamental relationship between voltage, current and resistance.

Ohm's law relates voltage, current and resistance.
V = IR
Therefore:
I = V/R
R = V/I

You can experiment with this relationship in the Electrical & Electronics Laboratory.
CONTROL

PID Control

Understand proportional, integral and derivative control.

A PID controller calculates a control action from the error between a desired setpoint and the measured output.
u(t)=Kp·e(t)+Ki∫e(t)dt+Kd·de(t)/dt
Kp responds to present error.
Ki responds to accumulated error.
Kd responds to the rate of change of error.

Open the Control Lab to experiment with these parameters.
ROBOTICS

Robot Kinematics

Learn how joint angles determine the position of a robotic mechanism.

Forward kinematics determines the position of the robot end-effector from known joint angles. For a two-link planar arm:
x=L₁cosθ₁+L₂cos(θ₁+θ₂)
y=L₁sinθ₁+L₂sin(θ₁+θ₂)
Inverse kinematics works in the opposite direction: it determines the joint angles required to reach a target position.
AUTOMOTIVE

Vehicle Dynamics

Understand the forces that determine vehicle acceleration and speed.

Vehicle acceleration depends on the difference between driving force and resistance forces.
Fnet = Ftraction − Fdrag − Frolling − Fgrade
Aerodynamic drag can be approximated by:
Fdrag = ½ρCdAv²
This model is used inside the MECH-X Automotive Laboratory.
ELECTRONICS

RC Time Constant

Understand the charging behaviour of a resistor and capacitor circuit.

The RC time constant describes how quickly a capacitor charges or discharges.
τ = RC
During charging:
Vc(t)=V(1−e⁻ᵗ/τ)
After approximately one time constant, the capacitor reaches about 63.2% of its final voltage.
MECHATRONICS

What is Mechatronics?

Discover how mechanical, electrical and software engineering work together.

Mechatronics combines multiple engineering disciplines to create intelligent systems. A typical mechatronic system may contain:

• Mechanical components
• Sensors
• Actuators
• Electronics
• Control algorithms
• Embedded software

Examples include industrial robots, autonomous vehicles, CNC machines, drones and automated manufacturing systems.
