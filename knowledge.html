A DC motor converts electrical energy into mechanical rotational energy. The applied voltage produces current in the armature, creating electromagnetic torque.

Important relationships include:

  • Back EMF: E = Keω
  • Motor torque: T = KtI
  • Mechanical power: P = Tω

A PID controller uses proportional, integral and derivative actions to reduce the difference between a desired setpoint and the measured output.

  • Kp responds to present error.
  • Ki responds to accumulated error.
  • Kd responds to the rate of change of error.
Open Control Lab

Kinematics studies the position, velocity and motion of robotic mechanisms without directly considering the forces causing the motion.

Forward kinematics calculates the end-effector position from known joint angles. Inverse kinematics calculates joint angles required to reach a desired position.

Open Robotics Lab

Stress describes the internal force distributed over an area of a material.

Normal stress is calculated using:

σ = F / A

Strain represents deformation relative to the original dimension:

ε = ΔL / L

Vehicle dynamics examines how forces acting on a vehicle affect acceleration, speed, handling and motion.

  • Aerodynamic drag
  • Rolling resistance
  • Road grade force
  • Engine and drivetrain force
Open Automotive Lab

Ohm's Law describes the relationship between voltage, current and resistance in an electrical circuit.

V = IR

Therefore:

  • I = V/R
  • R = V/I
Open Electrical Lab

A gear system transfers rotational motion and torque between shafts. Gear ratio describes the relationship between the numbers of teeth on two gears.

Gear Ratio = Driven Teeth / Driver Teeth

Increasing the reduction ratio generally decreases output speed while increasing available output torque, subject to losses.

Open Mechanical Lab

Beam deflection is the displacement of a beam caused by an applied load.

δ = FL³ / 3EI

For a cantilever beam with a point load at its free end, the equation depends on the beam geometry, material stiffness and loading condition.

Open Mechanical Lab

An RC circuit contains a resistor and capacitor. Its time response depends on the time constant.

τ = RC

During charging, capacitor voltage approaches the supply voltage exponentially.
